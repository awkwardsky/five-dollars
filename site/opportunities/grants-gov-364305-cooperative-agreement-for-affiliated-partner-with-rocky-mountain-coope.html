Source summary

The U.S. Geological Survey (USGS) is offering a funding opportunity to a CESU partner for research in updating a production pipeline for producing species distribution models to detect plant species habitat life history information about target species to inform targeted environmental predictors including those derived from remotely sensed imagery. The research will be used to further develop an updated modeling software and improve our ability to detect plant species' habitat to deliver to practitioners, ensuring they have the best available science to inform decisions (EO 14303 – Restoring Gold Standard Science (May 23, 2025). Species distribution models (SDMs) are useful in informing management actions for both problematic invasive and desirable native plant species by predicting habitat suitability. There is a need to be able to detect where species' habitat is across large geographic extents at a fine resolution at low abundance to be able to inform management actions. For example, there are landscape scale examples of habitat suitability models that overpredict species' potential distributions and therefore require larger areas to be of concern for rare plant species. There is a need to develop models at finer resolutions, including using novel environmental predictors derived from satellites such as sentinel. The pipeline currently being used for INHABIT and rare plant modeling efforts is outdated and requires updates to include current best practices for modeling. These updates must be compatible with ongoing data production pipelines, risk assessments, and decision support systems and tools to successfully determine where on the landscape species may be found.
